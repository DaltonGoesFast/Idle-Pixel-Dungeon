Game Design Document: Tactical Idle
Dungeon Crawler (Working Title)
1. High Concept & Core Pillars
A hybrid semi-idle dungeon management RPG and deterministic turn-based tactical
roguelite loosely based on Shattered Pixel Dungeon (SPD) and Into the Breach.
  ●​ Macro-Preparation Over Corridor Walking: Empty hallway navigation is replaced with
      UI-driven exploration, shifting player focus to loadout prep, resource management, and
      high-stakes tactical combat rooms.
  ●​ Deterministic Tactical Puzzles: Combat features zero RNG accuracy or damage rolls.
      Every battle takes place on a compact grid where positioning, turn economy, terrain
      manipulation, and telegraphed attacks decide the outcome.
  ●​ Shared Camp, Individual Climbs: Four distinct classes share a persistent Camp, Stash,
      and Crafting ecosystem, but level up and earn Strength individually.

2. The Core Gameplay Loop
Phase A: The Surface (Camp & Maintenance)
Between runs, the player operates out of a persistent Surface Camp:
  ●​ Crafting & Upgrading: Spend raw materials (Iron Ore, Dark Gold, Seeds, Arcane Resin)
     gathered from runs to forge weapons/armor, brew potions, and upgrade Camp facilities.
  ●​ Loadout Prep: Equip heroes from the shared Stash, stock their supply pack (Rations and
     Torches), and select consumables for the target biome.
  ●​ Identification Queue: Unidentified rings, wands, and curios brought back from the
     dungeon are placed in the Enchanter's Desk to identify over time.

Phase B: Active Expeditions (UI-Driven Delving)
  ●​ The Delve Bar: Exploration is driven by a UI progress bar ticking from 0% to 100% per
     floor. As it ticks, Torchlight and Satiety slowly drain, and the hero passively gathers minor
     gold and dew drops.
  ●​ Encounter Triggers: Every 20–25% of floor progress, the Delve Bar pauses and draws
     an encounter or presents a Door Choice (e.g., Door A: Smells of Ozone [Shaman Battle /
     Wand Reward] vs. Door B: Locked [Costs 1 Iron Key / Crystal Chest Event]).
  ●​ UI Special Rooms: Classic SPD puzzle rooms (Piranha rooms, Sacrificial Fires,
     Animated Statues, Barricades) appear as interactive UI cards where players spend
     consumables, tools, or HP to claim rewards or initiate optional fights.
  ●​ Extraction vs. Greed: Players can extractback to Camp with 100% of their backpack loot
     at Campfire Floors (every 5th floor after a boss) or by burning a Scroll of Passage.

Phase C: Offline Patrols (The Idle Engine)
  ●​ Any unlocked hero not currently in an active expedition—and the active hero when the
     game is closed—can be assigned to a multi-hour Offline Patrol in any biome they have
     previously cleared.
  ●​ Based on the hero's equipped stats relative to the biome's stat gate, they passively
     accumulate steady upgrade fuel (Iron Ore, Seeds, Dew, Arcane Resin, and occasional
     un-ID'd curios) without advancing floor records or fighting bosses.

3. Deterministic Tactical Combat System
The Arena & Turn Economy
  ●​ Micro-Grid Arenas: Combat triggers transition the UI into a compact 8x8 or 10x10 grid
     filled with tactical terrain (doors, tall grass, chasms, water, and traps).
  ●​ Solo Hero Focus: Each expedition is fought by a single hero on the grid, keeping turns
     fast and true to Shattered Pixel Dungeon.
  ●​ 1.0 Turn Tick System: Uses SPD's deterministic turn clock. Standard movement and
     attacks take 1.0 tick; fast enemies (e.g., Sewer Crabs) move at 0.5 ticks; heavy weapons
     swing at 1.5 ticks.

Fixed Math & The "Guard-Break" Armor System
All player and enemy attacks deal fixed damage with 100% accuracy. Defense is governed by
the interplay between Flat Damage Reduction (DR) and Barrier:
   ●​ Armor Value = Flat DR: Reduces incoming physical attack damage by a flat integer.
   ●​ Barrier (Shielding): Heroes start each combat room with a set amount of Barrier
       (augmented by armor tier, Warrior's Broken Seal, or potions).
   ●​ The Chip Rule: Any attack dealing greater than 0 damage after DR reduces Barrier first,
       then HP. However, any attack that is 100% blocked by DR still deals 1 minimum chip
       damage to Barrier.
   ●​ Tactical Implication: Swarming weak enemies (Rats, Flies, Crabs) are dangerous
       because they strip a tank's Barrier in a single turn, leaving the hero vulnerable to
       armor-piercing magic, bleed effects, or heavy hitters waiting behind them.

Positional Mechanics & Telegraphs
  ●​ Surprise Attacks: Kiting an enemy through a doorway or around a single tile of tall grass
     triggers a guaranteed Surprise Attack (dealing bonus damage, applying knockback, or
     bypassing enemy DR).
  ●​ Telegraphed Specials: Standard melee attacks resolve on contact, while heavy attacks,
     ranged shots, and boss abilities place visible Red Danger Tiles on the grid that resolve
     after a set tick count, allowing players to sidestep or shove enemies into the line of fire.
  ●​ Environmental Combos: Knocking enemies into chasms causes instant death (halving
     their loot drop, collectable on the next floor's UI); water conducts lightning across
     connected tiles; fire spreads across grass tiles.

Biome Stat Gates & Hand-Me-Down Progression
The dungeon is divided into 5 Biomes, each acting as a strict stat gate:
  1.​ Sewers (Floors 1–5): Target 10–11 Str | Tier 1–2 Gear
  2.​ Prison (Floors 6–10): Target 12–13 Str | Tier 2–3 Gear
  3.​ Caves (Floors 11–15): Target 14–15 Str | Tier 3–4 Gear
  4.​ Metropolis (Floors 16–20): Target 16–17 Str | Tier 4–5 Gear
  5.​ Demon Halls (Floors 21–25): Target 18+ Str | Tier 5 Endgame Gear
Because gear is shared at Camp while character levels are separate, a player pushing the
Caves with their Warrior can equip a newly unlocked Level 1 Mage (10 Str) with heavily
upgraded Tier-1/Tier-2 "hand-me-down" gear from the Camp Stash to rapidly power-level the
Mage through the Sewers.

5. Camp Economy & The 20-Scroll Capstone System
  ●​ Common Fuel vs. Capstone Blueprints: Common resources (Iron Ore, Seeds, Gold,
     Resin) are farmed infinitely to level up Camp facilities and forge gear. However, Camp
     facilities have hard upgrade caps that can only be broken using a Scroll of Upgrade
     (SoU).
  ●​ The 20 Scroll Supply: A Scroll of Upgrade drops the first time each Boss is defeated
     by each Class (5 Biomes × 4 Classes = 20 Scrolls total).
  ●​ Camp Facilities:
        ○​ Blacksmith (Fueled by Iron Ore): SoUs unlock higher upgrade caps (+1 → +2 →
           +3), higher gear tiers, and reduced Strength requirements on forged items.
        ○​ Alchemy Pot (Fueled by Seeds/Resin): SoUs unlock Exotic Potions, larger Potion
           Belts, and Elevator Starter Elixirs.
        ○​ Guild Hall (Fueled by Gold/Trophies): SoUs unlock longer Offline Patrol
           durations, larger backpacks, and Subclass Tomes.
        ○​ Enchanter's Desk (Fueled by Runestones/Scraps): SoUs unlock Ring/Artifact
           upgrading and Enchantment preservation.

6. Death & Recovery Mechanics
When a hero dies during an active expedition, consequences are punishing on the run level
while preserving account-level progression:
  1.​ Unbanked Loot Loss: 100% of raw materials and unbanked items in the backpack from
      that expedition are lost.
  2.​ Damaged Equipped Gear: The Weapon, Armor, and Rings the hero was wearing are
      returned to the Camp Blacksmith in a Cracked state. They cannot be used until repaired
      with stockpiled Iron Ore.
  3.​ Hero Level & Strength Reset: The fallen hero wakes up at Camp reset to Level 1 and
      10 Strength.
  4.​ The Re-Climb & Stepping Stone Gear: Because heroes reset to 10 Str on death,
      players maintain dedicated "Leveling Sets" (e.g., +2 Cloth Armor and +2 Shortsword) at
      Camp to breeze back through early biomes and regain their Strength potions—or spend
      Alchemy resources on Starter Elixirs at the Dungeon Elevator to boost a reset hero
      straight to the Strength/Level baseline of a deeper biome.

7. Open Design Questions (To Be Decided)
The following systems and specifications still need to be finalized during pre-production:

A. UI Layout & Target Platform
  ●​ Screen Architecture: Will the game run in a unified dashboard (where Camp tabs and
     the Delve/Combat window share the screen) or transition between full-screen states
     (Camp Screen → Delve Overlay → Combat Grid)?
  ●​ Aspect Ratio & Input: Is the primary target desktop landscape (16:9), mobile portrait
     (9:16), or adaptable for dual-screen/foldable layouts?

B. Baseline Combat Math (Biome 1 Balancing)
  ●​ Exact Number Scale: Establish the baseline numbers for Floor 1–5 (e.g., Does a Level 1
     Warrior start with 20 HP, 3 Barrier, 1 DR, and deal 3 damage? How much HP/DR do
     Sewer Rats, Crabs, and Gnolls have?).
  ●​ Barrier Refresh Rate: Does Barrier refresh to 100% automatically at the start of every
     combat room, or does it regenerate partially (e.g., +50% per room) to create attrition
     across a floor?
  ●​ Surprise Attack Formula: Should deterministic Surprise Attacks grant flat bonus
     damage (+50%), ignore enemy DR, or apply a 1-tile knockback/stun?
